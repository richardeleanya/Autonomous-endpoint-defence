# P2.2 Enforcement Controller — Design Specification

**Accountable owner:** Endpoint Runtime Engineering Lead. **Responsible team:** Endpoint Runtime Engineering, including Windows and Linux adapter maintainers. Security Engineering reviews policy and approval boundaries. Control Plane Engineering owns lease issuance, operator approval and reconciliation. QA owns the evidence pack. CTO owns the P2 gate decision. These are role assignments.

## 1. Execution authority

The node enforcement controller is the sole product path to privileged containment and recovery adapters. P2.1 evaluates policy and returns a decision without side effects. The proposer, investigator, mesh and control plane cannot invoke an adapter. The control plane issues scoped authorisation and records approvals. The node makes the final execution decision against current local state.

Before invoking an adapter, the controller requires an effective ALLOW for the exact tenant, device, incident, action, target and policy version. It validates the signed decision and scoped execution authorisation, checks current mode, trust, expiry, revocation, approval, protected-target and budget requirements, and durably records intent and consumes the relevant authorisation and budget. Observe-only prevents all privileged product actions. A missing or uncertain prerequisite denies execution. A denial or downgrade cannot become an adapter call.

Duplicate delivery, concurrent delivery and redelivery after restart cannot create another privileged effect for the same decision. When a restart leaves the result uncertain, the controller holds the reservation, reconciles observable state and escalates ambiguity to an operator. It does not retry a privileged effect blindly. Audit links the proposal, decision, applicable lease and approval, reservation, execution outcome and recovery verification.

## 2. Degraded operation and lease ownership

Only the partitioned state can use a previously issued offline lease, and only for a pre-authorised local T2 action that can satisfy all policy predicates without an unavailable dependency. Cached peer attestations cannot substitute for a required fresh quorum. Fully offline, suspected control plane and suspected node deny T2+. Unknown or conflicting state denies T2+. The P2.5 offline integration profile remains limited to T0/T1 even when the production design would permit leased T2.

Control Plane Engineering issues signed leases while online. Each lease binds tenant, device, permitted action and target scope, policy version, absolute expiry and maximum spend. The issuer reserves the slice against the tenant fleet cap. The node verifies lease scope, authority, expiry, applicable revocation state and trusted time. Expired leases, unreliable time, unavailable required revocation state and exhausted budgets deny execution. No local component issues, extends or replenishes a lease while partitioned.

The issuer keeps outstanding allocations and unresolved reservations in its cap accounting through partition and reconciliation. The node reports durable spend on reconnect and the control plane reconciles idempotently before returning confirmed unused capacity or authorising a replacement. A mismatch suspends further privileged execution for that device pending review. A kill switch stops issuance immediately and stops execution on nodes that receive it. Disconnected nodes cannot observe a new revocation until contact resumes. Absolute expiry and revocation freshness rules bound that exposure.

## 3. A-18 recovery approval

A-18 restore_file remains T3 and recovery class R3 (backup-dependent). A tenant-authorised operator opens a recovery request and records the incident, device, destination, backup identity and verification result. A distinct tenant-authorised approver using MFA reviews the exact restore scope and evidence. Approval is fresh, signed, single-use and bound to the request, target, backup and policy version. The controller also requires the separately signed proposal and normal policy gates. A backup being available never constitutes approval.

The product can perform the restore only online. If the product cannot safely authorise or verify the restore, the customer's incident commander routes it to an authorised local administrator through the customer's manual recovery runbook. That path uses separate recorded approval and backup validation, preserves available incident evidence, and records the outcome in the case when access returns. It does not enable the product adapter during an outage. The recovery verifier checks restored integrity and records success, failure or escalation.
